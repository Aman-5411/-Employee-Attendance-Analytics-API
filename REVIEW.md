# REVIEW.md

Defects found in the starter `app/main.py` (helpers and endpoints), and how each was fixed.

| # | Where (function / line) | What is wrong | How you'd notice it (test, input, or symptom) | How you fixed it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `compute_late_minutes` | The grace boundary is easy to get wrong. Late must be *strictly more than* 10:00 after `shift_start`, and the minutes must be counted from `shift_start`, not from the end of the grace period. | Shift 09:30: `09:40:00` must give 0, `09:40:01` must give 10, `10:15:59` must give 45. A `>=` check or "minutes minus 10" fails these. | Work in whole seconds: return 0 when `elapsed <= 600`, otherwise `elapsed // 60`. The three examples are in `test_helpers.py`. |
| 2 | `compute_work_hours` | Float `round()` is not half-up. `round(4.005, 2)` gives `4.0` because of binary representation, and banker's rounding goes to even. | 14418 s is exactly 4.005 h and must be reported as `4.01`. | Use `Decimal` with `ROUND_HALF_UP`. `half_day` is then decided on this rounded value (`< 4.50`, R5), not on the raw float. |
| 3 | `compute_overtime` | Overtime must be floor minutes from `shift_end` and only counted if it is at least 30. For an overnight shift the end falls on the *next* calendar day, so measuring against the record's own date gives a negative or huge number. | Day shift 09:30-18:30: punch-out 18:59 gives 0, 19:00 gives 30. Overnight 22:00-06:00 started on the 6th: punch-out 06:40 on the 7th gives 40. | Build the shift-end instant in IST. Add one day when `shift_end <= shift_start`. Floor the difference and apply the 30-minute threshold. |
| 4 | `shift_start_for_punch`, `punch_in`, `regularize_attendance` (attendance date) | A plain `punch_in.date()` is wrong for overnight shifts, and it is wrong in UTC. A punch at 02:00 IST belongs to the previous day's shift on a 22:00-06:00 roster. | Punch in at 21:55 IST for a 22:00-06:00 shift, or at 03:00 IST (still the previous shift). The stored `date` must be the day the shift started. | Convert to IST first. If the shift is overnight and the IST time is earlier than `shift_end`, subtract one day (R1). The same rule is reused when a corrected `punch_in` is validated against the record's date. |
| 5 | `truncate_to_seconds`, `as_utc` | Instants must be cut to whole seconds before any calculation, and the cut value is what is stored and returned. PyMongo hands back naive datetimes by default, and mixing naive and aware values raises `TypeError` or silently shifts by 5.5 h. | `punched_at` = `...40_900` ms must behave as `09:40:00`. Subtracting a stored naive datetime from an aware one crashes with a 500. | The client is created with `tz_aware=True`. Every datetime goes through `as_utc` (naive is treated as UTC). Truncation happens at the API boundary, before any computation. |
| 6 | `PunchInIn`, `PunchOutIn`, `RegularizeIn` (timestamp validation) | Anything numeric was accepted as a timestamp: seconds, floats, strings, booleans. Out-of-range values then crash `datetime.fromtimestamp` and return a 500. | `punched_at: 1783312500` (seconds), `1.7833125e12`, `"1783312500000"`, `true`. All must be 422. | `StrictInt` plus `validate_epoch_ms`, which enforces the contract range (100000000000 to 4102444800000). |
| 7 | `punch_in` (race) | Check-then-insert lets two simultaneous requests both pass the check, giving two records or two 201s. | Fire two identical punch-ins in parallel. Expect one 201 and one 409. | Rely on the unique index `(emp_code, date)` and catch `DuplicateKeyError` to return 409. There is no read before the write. |
| 8 | `punch_out` (race) | Reading the record, checking `punch_out is None` and then updating lets two parallel punch-outs both succeed. | Two parallel punch-outs on one open record. Expect exactly one 200. | The update filter includes `punch_out: None` and `_id`, and the result is checked with `modified_count`. The loser gets 409. |
| 9 | `punch_out` (record lookup) | Looking the record up by "today's date" breaks across midnight. The shift started yesterday. | Overnight punch-in on the 6th at 21:55, punch-out on the 7th at 06:40 returns 404 instead of 200. | Find the employee's latest record with `punch_in <= punched_at`, as the contract says. Records with null `punch_in` (ABSENT or LEAVE) never match. Also enforce 422 when `punched_at <= punch_in` or more than 24 h later. |
| 10 | `regularize_attendance` (history) | Overwriting `history`, or logging fields that did not change, destroys the audit trail. Two concurrent corrections can also lose one entry. | Correct a record twice. Both entries must exist. A PATCH that changes nothing must be 422. History `punch_in` values must be epoch ms. | Recompute all derived fields from the final times, diff old vs new, record only real changes, and `$push` exactly one entry. The write is guarded on the state that was read, so a concurrent edit gets 409 instead of overwriting. |
| 11 | `regularize_attendance` (status rules) | ABSENT or LEAVE with punch times, a presence status with no `punch_in`, or a `punch_out` before `punch_in` must be rejected. Setting ABSENT or LEAVE must clear both punch times and reset the derived fields. | `PATCH {"status": "LEAVE", "punch_in": ...}` returns 422. `PATCH {"status": "ABSENT"}` on a closed record clears times, `work_hours`, overtime and `half_day`. | Explicit checks, each returning 422, before any write. |
| 12 | `attendance_for_response`, `employee_for_response` | Datetimes and `_id` leak into responses. `history[].changes.punch_in/punch_out` nest datetimes that a top-level conversion misses. Legacy records may lack `history`, `half_day` and `late_minutes`. | A response contains `_id` or an ISO date string. A seeded legacy record returns a 500 or a missing key. | Strip `_id`, convert top-level and nested instants to epoch ms, and default the missing fields. |
| 13 | `GET /attendance`, `GET /employees` (indexes and paging) | Without indexes the list endpoints scan the collection. Sorting `date desc, emp_code asc` needs a matching index. `total` must reflect the filters. | `explain` shows `COLLSCAN`, and latency grows with 100k records. | Indexes are created idempotently at startup (see DECISIONS.md). `total` uses the same filter as the page. `page_size` is capped at 100. |
| 14 | `EmployeeIn` (input validation) | Weak validation lets `shift_start == shift_end`, a malformed `emp_code` or a malformed shift time through. Derived fields sent by a client must be ignored. | `shift_start = shift_end = "09:30"` must be 422. `{"work_hours": 99}` in a body must have no effect. | Regex patterns, a `model_validator` for the shift rule and `extra="ignore"`. Uniqueness is enforced by the unique index (409), not by a prior lookup. |
| 15 | Connection config | A hard-coded URI, or a `.env` that overrides real environment variables, fails against the grader's injected values. | The app connects to the wrong database in the grader. | `os.getenv` with `load_dotenv()`, which does not override variables that are already set. |

## Looked at and decided it is not a defect

- **`@app.on_event("startup")`** is deprecated in recent FastAPI but works, and index creation is idempotent. A lifespan handler would be cleaner, with no behaviour change.
- **`skip/limit` pagination** is fine at about 100k records because the sort is index-backed. It would not be fine at 100x the data (see DECISIONS.md).
- **Parsing `date` with `$dateFromString` and taking `$dayOfWeek`** is consistent. The string is a calendar date, parsed as UTC midnight, so the weekday is the same weekday.
- **`punch_in` accepts future timestamps.** The contract does not forbid it.
